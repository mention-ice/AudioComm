What is being sent

A frame has two parts. The control part is sent at 400 bit/s, at 4 / 8 kHz or hopping, two modes every receiver knows in advance: 0.1 s warm-up · MLS preamble · header. The message part uses the tones and rate chosen on the transmitter: coded and scrambled message · MLS postamble (the same sequence as the preamble). The header carries the type (text, image or room sounding) and size of the message, the code, the rate and the tones, protected by a CRC-8 and the convolutional code, so the receivers need no settings, as with the SIGNAL field of Wi-Fi. Bit 0 is a burst of the low tone, bit 1 a burst of the high tone; with 4 tones, each burst carries 2 bits (see below). audiocomm_v2.py produces and decodes the same frames.

The receivers find a frame by correlating what they hear with the three possible preambles (maximum-length sequences of 127, 255 or 511 bits). A longer preamble gathers more energy before deciding that a frame starts: each doubling of its length gives about 3 dB of margin, so phones farther away, or in a noisier room, still find the frame; the header is then sent 2 or 4 times so that it is read as far away. Neither helps once the message itself is too weak: for that, also lower the rate (200 bit/s gives the message 3 dB more) and use a code. The preamble also gives the instant of every symbol. Its long symbols (2.5 ms) tolerate an error of several samples, but the message's short ones do not (0.6 ms at 1600 bit/s). So with tone hopping, once the message is in, the receivers fine-tune its timing on the message itself, by up to half a symbol or 0.5 ms, and keep it if it makes the two tones of each symbol clearly more distinct.

The receivers read the code from the header, so only the transmitter chooses it. Hamming (7,4): 3 parity bits per 4 bits, corrects 1 wrong bit in each block of 7 (hard decisions). Convolutional: rate 1/2, constraint length 7 (generators 171, 133 octal), decoded by the Viterbi algorithm on the soft outputs. Turbo: rate 1/2 as in 4G: two small recursive convolutional codes, the second fed with the message shuffled, decoded by two soft decoders that exchange what they learnt, up to 8 times. It needs about 1.3 dB less signal than the convolutional code for the same frame error rate. Hamming, convolutional and turbo bits go through an interleaver, which spreads a burst of errors (an echo, a cough) over many code blocks. v0 also offered Reed–Solomon (255,223), which corrects up to 16 wrong bytes in each block of 255. In a room it fails as early as Hamming, since both decide each bit before decoding, so this page no longer sends it; it still decodes it.

Tone hopping. In a room, the sound of each bit rings on for a large part of a second, far longer than the bit (2.5 ms at 400 bit/s). With only two tones, the echo of the earlier bits sits on both tones all the time; beyond the critical distance, where the echo is louder than the direct sound, it buries the bit. Hopping spreads the bits over 6400 / R pairs of adjacent tones from 3.2 to 16 kHz (16 pairs at 400 bit/s, R the rate): bit k uses pair 7k mod 16 (s k mod H in general), so a pair is used again only H bits later and the echo on each tone is H/2 times weaker, about 3 dB per doubling of the pairs. The preamble and header hop too (at 400 bit/s), otherwise far receivers would not find the frame. Each tone has its own level (speaker, room, microphone), so the receiver levels the tones before deciding. The band starts at 3.2 kHz: below that, a small speaker gives little and a room is noisy, and in a real test the 1.6 kHz tone the band first started at caused nearly all the errors.

Four tones (v2). With 4 tones, a symbol carries 2 bits, Gray-coded so that neighbouring tones differ in one bit only. At the same bit rate it lasts twice as long and gathers twice the energy, and its tones, spaced by the symbol rate (half the bit rate), take the same band as the 2 tones of binary FSK. The receiver then compares 4 energies instead of 2, which non-coherent detection turns into a gain: in simulation, about 2 dB in noise and 2 to 3 dB in a room's echo, or roughly 25 to 40 % more distance, and 3200 bit/s becomes usable, where binary FSK would make errors even close to the speaker. Longer symbols also tolerate twice the timing error. The fixed tones are 4, 5.6, 7.2 and 8.8 kHz (or 8 to 12.8 kHz); with hopping, the 6400 / R pairs become groups of 4 adjacent tones. The control part stays binary, so every receiver finds the frame and reads the header; receivers older than v2 find no 4-tone mode in it and ignore the frame. With 4 tones the header's 4 rates are 400 to 3200 bit/s, and the postamble and the message are each padded to a whole number of symbols.

6400 bit/s. At 6400 bit/s, with 4 tones, a symbol lasts 0.31 ms (15 samples at 48 kHz) and its tones, spaced 3.2 kHz, are 3.2, 6.4, 9.6 and 12.8 kHz: one group fills the whole band, so there is nothing left to hop over, and the echo of the earlier symbols always lies on the same 4 tones. FSK can go no faster in this band, because sending one tone at a time carries at most half a bit per second per hertz; going further takes many tones at once, each with its own phase (OFDM, as in Wi-Fi). In simulation it needs about 4.7 dB more SNR and 3.6 dB more DRR than 3200 bit/s, so it reaches 60 to 70 % of that distance. Choosing 6400 selects Hopping, the only tone set that spans the band. The header marks it as tone set 3, which receivers made before it ignore.

Soft output

ln(E₁/E₀) compares the energies received on the two tones. Its sign is the right decision when both tones arrive equally strong, but its size says little about reliability: a bit drowned in noise can look as sure as a clean one.

Calibrated LLR measures, on the known bits of the postamble (sent at the message's tones and rate), the level A and the noise σ² of each tone, then uses the log-likelihood ratio of non-coherent FSK, ln I₀(A₁E₁/σ₁²) − A₁²/2σ₁² − ln I₀(A₀E₀/σ₀²) + A₀²/2σ₀². If the postamble is not found (for example when the end of the sound was cut off), A and σ² are estimated on the message itself: its bits are guessed from the energies, then the guesses and the estimates are refined in turn (EM). With the postamble, the LLR is then multiplied by the factor α ≤ 1 that best predicts the postamble's own bits: the echoes of short symbols have heavier tails than Gaussian noise, which makes the model too sure of itself (α ≈ 0.3 at 1600 bit/s in a real room). It corrects an imbalance between the tones (speaker, microphone, room) and gives the Viterbi decoder reliabilities on the right scale. With 4 tones, each tone m gets its own λm = ln I₀(AmEm/σm²) − Am²/2σm², the log-likelihood that it was the tone sent, and the LLR of each of the symbol's two bits is ln Σ eλ over the two tones where that bit is 1, minus the same over the two where it is 0; ln(E₁/E₀) becomes the strongest of the first two tones against the strongest of the other two. The energies are first divided by the local signal level (averaged over ±24 symbols), because in a room the main disturbance, the echo, rises and falls with the signal. Room sounding

Choose Sound the room on the transmitter. After the usual control part (preamble and header, so that the receivers know what follows) it plays a maximum-length sequence of 2n−1 samples of ±1 at 48 kHz, several times in a row. The circular autocorrelation of an MLS is a single spike, so correlating one received period with the sequence gives the impulse response h(t) of the whole chain: speaker, room and microphone. Averaging the periods lowers the noise; the first period is skipped, so that the room already rings in its steady state.

From h(t): the reverberation time RT60, the time the sound energy takes to fall by 60 dB, from the slope of the Schroeder energy decay curve (the backward integral of h²) between −5 and −25 dB (T20) or −15 dB (T10); the direct-to-reverberant ratio (energy within 2.5 ms of the direct sound against the rest); C50 (first 50 ms against the rest); the rms delay spread τrms, to compare with the duration of a bit (2.5 ms at 400 bit/s): echoes much longer than a bit cause intersymbol interference. The frequency response shows how strongly each tone arrives, and the noise heard just before the frame gives the SNR per frequency, turned into the Es/N0 a tone of the modem would get.

These are the parameters of a room model: a direct path, an exponentially decaying diffuse tail with the measured RT60, per-frequency gains and noise. audiocomm_v2.py sim plays any frame through a measured impulse response (downloaded above) to try the modem offline in that room.
