What is being sent

Frame: 0.1 s warm-up · 127-bit MLS preamble · 24-bit header (type, size and code, each bit sent 3 times) · coded and scrambled message · 127-bit MLS postamble. Bit 0 is a burst of the low tone, bit 1 a burst of the high tone. The same frame is produced by audiocomm.py, and by audioComm_tx.m without a code.

The receivers read the code from the header, so only the transmitter chooses it. Hamming (7,4): 3 parity bits per 4 bits, corrects 1 wrong bit in each block of 7 (hard decisions). Convolutional: rate 1/2, constraint length 7 (generators 171, 133 octal), decoded by the Viterbi algorithm on the soft outputs. Reed–Solomon (255,223): works on bytes, corrects up to 16 wrong bytes in each block of 255, good against bursts. Hamming and convolutional bits go through an interleaver, which spreads a burst of errors (an echo, a cough) over many code blocks.

Soft output

ln(E₁/E₀) compares the energies received on the two tones. Its sign is the right decision when both tones arrive equally strong, but its size says little about reliability: a bit drowned in noise can look as sure as a clean one.

Calibrated LLR measures, on the 127 known bits of the preamble, the level A and the noise σ² of each tone, then uses the log-likelihood ratio of non-coherent FSK, ln I₀(A₁E₁/σ₁²) − A₁²/2σ₁² − ln I₀(A₀E₀/σ₀²) + A₀²/2σ₀². It corrects an imbalance between the tones (speaker, microphone, room) and gives the Viterbi decoder reliabilities on the right scale. The energies are first divided by the local signal level (averaged over ±24 symbols), because in a room the main disturbance, the echo, rises and falls with the signal.
